\title{Align Only Where Needed: Alignment-Free Phoneme Scoring and Embedded CTC--Viterbi Stress Assessment}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Long, Nguyen Trung}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\texttt{long.nt270504@gmail.com}}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Phoneme scoring does not necessarily require phone boundaries, whereas
syllable-stress features do require temporal localization. We therefore
combine Cao et al.'s alignment-free Connectionist Temporal Classification
(CTC) vector features and a phone-only GOPT model with an embedded,
transcript-conditioned CTC--Viterbi path used only by a Mallela-inspired
sequential stress scorer. We compare this proposal on the official
speaker-disjoint SpeechOcean762 split with (i) a modular Montreal Forced
Aligner (MFA) pipeline and (ii) Gong et al.'s joint multi-task GOPT
architecture, adapted to the same CTC front end. Across five independent
seeds and 46,119 common test phones, the proposed phone branch obtains Pearson
correlation $0.645\mathbin{\pm}0.009$, versus $0.413\mathbin{\pm}0.013$ for
the MFA modular baseline and $0.418\mathbin{\pm}0.005$ for joint GOPT. On
2,354 common polysyllabic words, CTC--Viterbi and MFA yield stress-correctness
AUROC 0.681 and 0.662. Their canonical stress-location accuracies are 85.14\%
and 85.85\%; the paired difference is $-0.70$ percentage points (95\%
speaker-bootstrap interval $[-1.81,0.48]$), so no stress difference is
statistically resolved. Complete single-request CPU latency is 212.6 ms for
the proposal, 234.6 ms for modular MFA, and 192.5 ms for joint GOPT. Thus the
hybrid design is 1.10$\times$ faster than the like-for-like modular MFA
pipeline and substantially improves phone correlation, while the joint model
exposes a different speed--accuracy trade-off. We release reproducible feature,
training, paired-stress, latency, and official-checkpoint validation code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Computer-assisted pronunciation training benefits from segmental feedback
about individual phones and suprasegmental feedback about lexical stress.
Traditional goodness-of-pronunciation (GOP) pipelines first align a known
transcript, then aggregate acoustic evidence within each phone interval
\cite{witt2000phone,hu2015improved}. Stress systems likewise use syllable or
vowel regions to measure duration, intensity, fundamental frequency, and
spectral prominence \cite{fry1958experiments,yarra2019comparison}. This often
leads a complete assessment service to run an external forced aligner even when
only one branch truly needs boundaries.

Cao et al. recently showed that CTC permits phone-level pronunciation features
without selecting a frame path \cite{cao2024framework}. Their
alignment-free vector marginalizes over every CTC path for the canonical
sequence and locally modified alternatives. Conversely, the sequential stress
models of Mallela et al. operate on syllable features, so they still need
localized acoustic evidence \cite{mallela2024comparative}. Our design follows
both observations: do not align for the phone score, but derive stress regions
from a Viterbi path already available in the resident CTC posterior matrix.

The complete proposal has no MFA/Kaldi request-time dependency. Its Viterbi
trellis is nevertheless conditioned on the canonical phone sequence; we call
it \emph{embedded localization}, not unconstrained recognition. The relevant
engineering distinction is that the same CTC inference supplies both Cao
features and stress regions, without a second acoustic model, dictionary
pipeline, database, or alignment subprocess.

We evaluate three complete systems (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a strong modular MFA baseline with an MFA-aligned CTC vector phone
    scorer and an MFA-conditioned sequential stress scorer;
    \item the proposed Cao alignment-free phone scorer plus the same stress
    scorer using embedded CTC--Viterbi intervals; and
    \item Gong et al.'s published multi-aspect, multi-granularity GOPT
    architecture \cite{gong2022gopt}, adapted to emit both phone and word
    stress scores from the controlled MFA-aligned CTC input.
\end{enumerate}
The study reports a common held-out subset, five initialization seeds, a paired
speaker bootstrap for the fixed stress branches, and one-request latency with
equal thread/job settings. We also run the unmodified official GOPT checkpoint
on its released tensors to verify our interpretation of that architecture.

\section{Related Work}

Witt and Young introduced phone-level pronunciation confidence for interactive
language learning \cite{witt2000phone}; later DNN-GOP methods replaced
generative likelihoods with neural posteriors \cite{hu2015improved}.
SpeechOcean762 supplies a reproducible, expert-rated non-native English
benchmark and a Kaldi recipe \cite{zhang2021speechocean762}. MFA packages a
Kaldi-based text--speech alignment workflow for broader use
\cite{mcauliffe2017montreal,povey2011kaldi}.

Cao et al. formulate scalar and vector CTC-GOP features in alignment-based and
alignment-free forms \cite{cao2024framework}. Their strongest vector features
can be consumed by GOPT, a compact Transformer that combines acoustic GOP
features, canonical phone embeddings, learned position embeddings, and
phone-, word-, and utterance-level supervision \cite{gong2022gopt}. The
original GOPT input is an 84-dimensional Kaldi GOP vector. We retain its model
topology but use controlled CTC-derived vectors so the three systems share one
posterior source.

Lexical stress depends on relative syllabic cues rather than one absolute
measurement \cite{fry1958experiments}. Yarra et al. examine boundary sources
and contextual features for automatic stress detection
\cite{yarra2019comparison}. Mallela et al. model a word as a variable-length
sequence of syllables with recurrent networks and a post-processing constraint
that selects one primary stress \cite{mallela2024comparative}. Our local
stress checkpoint follows those architectural ideas. It is an adaptation, not
their unreleased ISLE checkpoint or an exact reproduction of their corpus
experiment.

\section{Three Assessment Systems}

\subsection{Shared CTC posterior matrix}

All systems use the Wav2Vec~2.0 XLSR-53 large phone recognizer released by Cao
et al., fine-tuned on LibriSpeech train-clean-100. It emits log posteriors
$\ell_{t,k}=\log p(k\mid\mathbf{x},t)$ for 39 ARPAbet phones plus CTC blank.
This common encoding is charged once to every complete pipeline. The
experimental branches differ after this matrix.

\subsection{Proposed alignment-free phone branch}

Let $\mathbf{y}=(y_1,\ldots,y_U)$ be the canonical phone sequence and
\begin{equation}
G(\mathbf{y})=-\log\sum_{\boldsymbol{\pi}:B(\boldsymbol{\pi})=\mathbf{y}}
\exp\sum_t \ell_{t,\pi_t}
\end{equation}
be its CTC negative log likelihood, where $B$ removes blanks and repeated
labels. For each canonical position $u$, Cao's vector concatenates
$G(\mathbf{y})$ with 40 local contrasts
\begin{equation}
r_{u,a}=G(\mathbf{y}_{u\leftarrow a})-G(\mathbf{y}),
\end{equation}
covering deletion and all 39 phone substitutions. The result is a 41-D vector
per phone and sums over all legal CTC paths; it contains no boundary estimate.
We implement the official ordering and verify its loss values against
exhaustive path enumeration on small lattices.

These vectors enter a phone-only GOPT: a linear feature projection, a 40-way
canonical-phone projection, learned positions, five learned class tokens,
three 24-dimensional one-head Transformer blocks, and a phone regression head.
The class tokens preserve the official topology even though auxiliary losses
are absent.

\subsection{Embedded CTC--Viterbi stress branch}

Stress features require regions. We expand the canonical sequence into
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}=(\phi,y_1,\phi,y_2,\ldots,\phi,y_U,\phi)
\end{equation}
and decode
\begin{equation}
V_t(s)=\ell_{t,z_s}+\max_{r\in\mathcal{P}(s)}V_{t-1}(r).
\end{equation}
The predecessor set permits a self-loop, one-state advance, and a two-state
skip into a phone only when adjacent labels differ. Repeated phones must pass
through blank. Backtracking provides one contiguous region per canonical phone
in $\mathcal{O}(TU)$ time. The implementation has explicit repeated-phone and
small-lattice tests.

Maximal-onset syllabification groups the phone regions, and the vowel interval
defines the nucleus. The local 42,061-parameter scorer consumes 38 features per
syllable (19 acoustic and 19 contextual), uses masked LSTM layers of 64, 32,
and 16 units, a four-dimensional attention layer, sigmoid scores, and a
word-level argmax. The comparison uses only this neural output
(\texttt{neural\_weight=1.0}); the deployed acoustic-prominence ensemble is
disabled.

\subsection{MFA modular baseline}

MFA 3.4.2 supplies word and phone intervals. For each phone interval we
average the 39 non-blank CTC log posteriors and concatenate 39 target-relative
log-posterior differences, yielding a 78-D aligned vector. A phone-only GOPT of
the same topology as above predicts the phone grade. The same fixed 38-D
stress feature code and sequential checkpoint use MFA rather than Viterbi
regions. Thus MFA is charged for one alignment, one CTC encoding, a phone head,
and the complete stress branch.

MFA phone sequences are globally matched to expert canonical sequences with
Levenshtein dynamic programming. Every diagonal match is retained, including
substitutions; an MFA interval is then labeled with the corresponding expert
canonical phone. Insertions and deletions alone are excluded. This avoids
artificially removing pronunciation errors merely because MFA chose a
different phone symbol.

\subsection{Joint GOPT comparator}

The third system uses the same MFA-aligned 78-D vectors but retains GOPT's
multi-task heads: phone accuracy; word accuracy, stress, and total; and five
utterance dimensions. It therefore produces both requested scores with one
model and no separate stress feature network. This is a controlled adaptation
of the published architecture, not the unchanged official Kaldi-front-end
pipeline. We validate the latter separately in Sec.~\ref{sec:official}.

\section{Experimental Design}

\subsection{Corpus, coverage, and training}

SpeechOcean762 contains 5,000 English utterances from 250 Mandarin-speaking
learners, split into speaker-disjoint train and test partitions of 2,500
utterances and 125 speakers each \cite{zhang2021speechocean762}. Five experts
provide phone accuracy grades from 0 to 2. Word stress is 10 when accepted and
5 when incorrect. We use the official split.

Cao features are available for 47,076 training and 47,369 test phones. MFA
exports 2,462 training and 2,447 test TextGrids with
\texttt{--ignore\_oovs}; diagonal mapping retains 46,064 and 46,119 phones,
respectively. All three phone systems are evaluated on the same 46,119 test
phones. The alignment-free model uses every available training phone, while
the MFA-dependent systems use 46,064. This 2.2\% coverage advantage is part of
the complete system comparison and a possible contributor to accuracy.

Feature families are normalized with one scalar mean and standard deviation
estimated only on their training examples. Models train for 100 epochs with
Adam ($10^{-3}$, $\beta_1=0.95$, $\beta_2=0.999$, weight decay
$5\times10^{-7}$), the released warm-up/multi-step schedule, and batch size
25. We report mean and sample standard deviation over five deterministic,
independent initializations; predictions are not ensembled.

\subsection{Endpoints and inference}

Phone endpoints are Pearson (PCC), Spearman (SRCC), and mean-squared error
(MSE) against expert phone grades. Run-matched PCC differences summarize model
initialization variation.

The stress set contains 2,354 common polysyllabic words from 1,578 utterances
and all 125 test speakers: 2,282 accepted-stress tokens and 72 stress errors.
Every system is evaluated on this same set. The primary common endpoint is area
under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) for accepted versus
incorrect stress. We additionally report PCC with the binary stress grade. For
the two sequential branches, canonical-location accuracy is meaningful among
words rated correct: when expert stress is accepted, a successful detector
should select the dictionary-primary syllable. GOPT emits a scalar word-stress
grade and does not produce a location.

For the two fixed sequential conditions, we resample speakers with replacement
10,000 times and recompute paired Viterbi-minus-MFA differences in AUROC and
location accuracy. These are uncertainty intervals, not a preregistered
non-inferiority test.

\subsection{Equal-resource latency}

Latency is measured on the same ARM64 macOS CPU, one request at a time. Every
system uses four PyTorch intra-operation threads; MFA uses exactly one job.
One CTC warm-up request is excluded. We record 100 complete front-end requests.
Stress time is measured over the paired set and amortized over all 2,447
MFA-aligned test requests, assigning zero to requests without a scored
polysyllable. A fresh one-job MFA corpus run takes 35.71 ms per requested
input, including failures. No multi-worker alignment-only timing enters the
complete comparison.

The proposal reduces complete latency by 22.06 ms, a 1.10$\times$ speedup over
modular MFA (Table~\ref{tab:latency}). Cao's 40 alternative CTC losses cost
more than reading MFA-aligned vector regions, and Viterbi adds 2.98 ms; both
remain cheaper than external MFA. Joint GOPT is fastest overall (1.22$\times$
the modular baseline) because its single head replaces the 42--46 ms
TensorFlow stress branch. It therefore offers the strongest speed and scalar
stress score here, while the proposal offers substantially higher phone
correlation and explicit stress location.

\subsection{Official GOPT checkpoint check}
\label{sec:official}

We ran the unmodified official model, checkpoint, normalization constants, and
released LibriSpeech test tensors. It obtains phone PCC 0.6183 over 47,369
phones and word-stress PCC 0.3245 over 15,967 words, closely matching the
repository values 0.616 and 0.326. This confirms the implementation path used
to derive our controlled model. Its 0.425 ms GOPT-head latency excludes the
Kaldi GOP/alignment front end and is not used in Table~\ref{tab:latency}.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The results support a division of labor. Marginalizing every CTC path is highly
effective for phone scoring; selecting one canonical path is sufficient to
localize syllable features without a resolved stress penalty. This is more
precise than proposing Viterbi as a universally superior alignment or phone
score. The third system also matters: external alignment does not automatically
make a complete system slower if a joint head removes a costly downstream
network.

Several limitations bound the claim. First, the local stress checkpoint is
Mallela-inspired rather than their ISLE model, and SpeechOcean supplies only a
correct/incorrect word grade, not the realized destination of an incorrect
stress. Second, only 72 common polysyllabic words carry a stress-error label;
AUROC is consequently uncertain. Third, the alignment-free phone model sees
1,012 more training phones than MFA-dependent models. That coverage is a real
system advantage but weakens a feature-only causal interpretation. Fourth, the
controlled GOPT adaptation uses 78-D CTC vectors rather than its original 84-D
Kaldi GOP front end, so its SpeechOcean row should not be compared directly
with official LibriSpeech performance. Fifth, MFA is a baseline interval source,
not manual boundary ground truth, and Viterbi timestamps should not be read as
articulatory truth.

Finally, latency depends on hardware, implementation, batching, and
parallelism. We intentionally compare one request, four common compute threads,
and one MFA job. A production MFA pool or a batched CTC service would answer a
different throughput question. Formal stress non-inferiority would also
require a prespecified margin and a corpus with substantially more stress
errors.

\section{Conclusion}

We implemented the intended hybrid pipeline: Cao et al.'s alignment-free vector
features score phones, while a transcript-conditioned CTC--Viterbi path exists
only to provide regions for a Mallela-inspired stress network. Against a
complete modular MFA baseline, this design improves phone PCC from
$0.413\pm0.013$ to $0.645\pm0.009$, shows no statistically resolved stress
difference, and lowers single-request latency from 234.6 to 212.6 ms. A
controlled joint-GOPT comparator is faster and stronger on scalar stress but
substantially weaker on phone correlation and lacks stress location. The
evidence therefore supports alignment only where the output requires it, with
the claim restricted to the measured models, corpus, and execution protocol.
